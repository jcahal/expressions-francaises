# Expressions françaises: the phrase set
#
# Format
#   @ tier | theme-id | Theme title           starts a section
#   French | English | Literal | Spoken | Register | Notes | Pattern | Swaps
#
# Trailing fields may be left out. Register codes:
#   N = neutral (safe anywhere)   F = formal   Fam = casual   A = slang
# Pattern + Swaps turn a phrase into a reusable frame:
#   Pattern "Je voudrais + [nom / infinitif]"  Swaps "un café · réserver une table"

@ 1 | salutations | Greetings & goodbyes
Bonjour. | Hello. / Good morning. | Good day. | | N | The default greeting until about 6 pm. Say it when you walk into any shop, bus, office or waiting room. Skipping it is genuinely rude in France.
Bonsoir. | Good evening. | | | N | From late afternoon on. Works as hello and, less often, as goodbye.
Salut ! | Hi! / Bye! | | | Fam | Hello AND goodbye among friends. Not for strangers or shopkeepers.
Coucou ! | Hey there! | Peekaboo! | | Fam | Warm, playful greeting for friends and family. Everywhere in texts.
Comment allez-vous ? | How are you? | How go you? | | F | Uses "vous": strangers, older people, work, or more than one person.
Comment ça va ? | How's it going? | How does it go? | | N | The everyday version. Usually cut down to "Ça va ?" with rising intonation.
Ça va ? — Ça va, et toi ? | You OK? — Good, and you? | It goes? — It goes, and you? | | Fam | The most common exchange in French. The same two words are the question and the answer; only the intonation changes.
Ça va bien, merci. | I'm doing well, thanks. | It goes well, thanks. | | N | Other answers: "Très bien" (very well), "Pas mal" (not bad), "Bof" (meh), "Ça va pas trop" (not great).
Pas mal, et vous ? | Not bad, and you? | | | N | Always bounce the question back: "et vous ?" (formal) or "et toi ?" (casual).
Enchanté(e). | Nice to meet you. | Enchanted. | | N | Add the -e in writing if you're a woman. It sounds the same.
Ça fait longtemps ! | Long time no see! | That makes a long time! | | N
Quoi de neuf ? | What's new? | What of new? | | Fam | Typical answer: "Pas grand-chose." (Not much.)
Pas grand-chose. | Not much. | Not a big thing. | | N | The standard reply to "Quoi de neuf ?"
Au revoir. | Goodbye. | Until seeing again. | | N | Safe with anyone.
Bonne journée ! | Have a nice day! | Good day! | | N | The standard goodbye when you leave a shop or café. You'll say it ten times a day.
Bonne soirée ! | Have a good evening! | | | N | The evening version of "Bonne journée". Also "Bonne fin de journée" (enjoy the rest of your day).
Merci, vous aussi ! | Thanks, you too! | Thanks, you also! | | N | The reply to any "Bonne journée / soirée / fin de journée". Casual: "Merci, toi aussi !"
À plus tard ! | See you later! | To later! | À plus ! / À+ | Fam | "À plus" is pronounced with the final s: "a-plooss". Written "À+" in texts.
À tout à l'heure ! | See you in a bit! | At all at the hour! | À t'à l'heure ! | N | Only when you'll see them again later the same day.
À bientôt ! | See you soon! | To soon! | | N | No fixed date.
À demain ! | See you tomorrow! | To tomorrow! | | N | À + a time means "see you then". | À + [moment] | À lundi · À ce soir · À la semaine prochaine · À samedi
Bon week-end ! | Have a good weekend! | | | N | Starts being said on Thursday afternoon.
Bonne nuit. | Good night. | | | N | Only when someone is going to bed. To say goodbye in the evening use "Bonne soirée".

@ 1 | politesse | Politeness & social glue
S'il vous plaît. | Please. | If it pleases you. | | N | Formal or plural. Also how you call a waiter: "S'il vous plaît !"
S'il te plaît. | Please. (casual) | If it pleases you. | S'te plaît / stp | Fam | With friends, family and kids.
Merci beaucoup. | Thank you very much. | Thanks a lot. | | N | Never "merci très beaucoup". Stronger: "Merci mille fois" (thanks a thousand times).
De rien. | You're welcome. | Of nothing. | | N | The most common reply to "merci".
Il n'y a pas de quoi. | Don't mention it. | There is nothing of which. | Y'a pas de quoi. | N | In speech "il" and "ne" disappear: "Y'a pas d'quoi."
Je vous en prie. | You're welcome. / Please, go ahead. | I pray you of it. | | F | Very polite. Also means "after you" or "help yourself". Casual: "Je t'en prie."
Avec plaisir. | With pleasure. / My pleasure. | | | N | To accept an offer, or as a warm reply to thanks.
Volontiers. | Gladly. / I'd love to. | Willingly. | | N | An elegant way to accept: "Un café ? — Volontiers !"
Non merci, ça ira. | No thanks, I'm fine. | No thanks, it will go. | | N | A polite refusal. Careful: "Merci" on its own, said to an offer, often means "no thanks".
Excusez-moi. | Excuse me. | | | N | To get someone's attention or squeeze past. Casual: "Excuse-moi."
Pardon. | Sorry. / Pardon me. / Sorry, what? | | | N | When you bump into someone, need to get by, or didn't hear.
Désolé(e). | Sorry. | | | N | A real apology. Stronger: "Je suis vraiment désolé(e)."
Je suis vraiment désolé(e). | I'm really sorry. | | Chuis vraiment désolé. | N | "Je suis" squashes into "chuis" in fast speech.
Ce n'est pas grave. | It's no big deal. / Don't worry about it. | It's not serious. | C'est pas grave. | N | The universal reply to an apology. Spoken French drops "ne" almost always.
Pas de souci. | No worries. | No worry. | | N | Everywhere. Also "Pas de problème" and "Aucun souci".
Après vous. | After you. | | | N | Holding a door. Casual: "Après toi."
Bon appétit ! | Enjoy your meal! | Good appetite! | | N | Said before eating. Reply: "Merci, vous aussi / toi aussi."
Santé ! | Cheers! | Health! | | N | Also "À la vôtre !" (formal) / "À la tienne !" (casual). Look people in the eye as you clink, or it's seven years of bad luck.
Félicitations ! | Congratulations! | | | N
Bonne chance ! | Good luck! | | | N
Bon courage ! | Hang in there! / Good luck with that! | Good courage! | | N | Wishes strength for something tiring: exams, work, moving house. Hugely common, no real English equivalent.
À vos souhaits ! | Bless you! | To your wishes! | | N | After a sneeze. Casual: "À tes souhaits !"

@ 1 | comprendre | Understanding & keeping the conversation going
Je ne comprends pas. | I don't understand. | | J'comprends pas. | N
Je n'ai pas compris. | I didn't catch that. | I have not understood. | J'ai pas compris. | N | Often more natural than the present tense: you didn't get what was just said.
Vous pouvez répéter, s'il vous plaît ? | Could you repeat that, please? | You can repeat? | | N | Casual: "Tu peux répéter ?"
Vous pouvez parler plus lentement ? | Could you speak more slowly? | You can speak more slowly? | | N | The most useful request a learner can make. | Vous pouvez + [infinitif] ? | répéter · parler plus fort · l'écrire · me montrer
Comment ? | Sorry? / Pardon? | How? | | N | The polite "what?" when you didn't hear. "Quoi ?" is blunt.
Qu'est-ce que ça veut dire ? | What does that mean? | What is it that that wants to say? | | N | "Vouloir dire" = to mean.
Ça veut dire quoi, « … » ? | What does "…" mean? | That wants to say what? | | Fam | Spoken French puts the question word at the end.
Comment on dit « … » en français ? | How do you say "…" in French? | How does one say…? | | N | Your lifeline. Use it constantly.
Comment ça s'écrit ? | How do you spell that? | How does that write itself? | | N
Je parle un peu français. | I speak a little French. | | | N
J'apprends le français. | I'm learning French. | I learn French. | | N | French has no "-ing" tense. The present covers both "I learn" and "I'm learning".
Vous parlez anglais ? | Do you speak English? | | | N | Asking in French first earns a lot of goodwill.
Je cherche le mot… | I'm looking for the word… | | | N | Buys you time mid-sentence. People will help you.
C'est comme… | It's like… | | | N | Describe what you can't name: "C'est comme un sac, mais plus petit."
Vous voulez dire… ? | You mean…? | You want to say…? | | N | To check you understood. Casual: "Tu veux dire… ?"
D'accord, je vois. | OK, I see. | Of agreement, I see. | | N | Shows you're following.
Attendez, je réfléchis. | Hang on, let me think. | Wait, I reflect. | | N | Casual: "Attends, je réfléchis."
Un instant, s'il vous plaît. | One moment, please. | | | N
Euh… | Um… | | | N | The French hesitation sound. Use it instead of English "um" and you'll sound far more native.
Comment dire… | How can I put it… | How to say… | | N | A classic thinking filler.
C'est-à-dire ? | Meaning? / What do you mean? | That is to say? | | N | Asks someone to explain or expand.
Pardon, j'ai pas entendu. | Sorry, I didn't hear. | Sorry, I have not heard. | | Fam

@ 1 | presentation | Introducing yourself
Je m'appelle… | My name is… | I call myself… | | N
Comment vous appelez-vous ? | What's your name? | How do you call yourself? | | F | Casual: "Tu t'appelles comment ?"
Tu t'appelles comment ? | What's your name? (casual) | You call yourself how? | | Fam | The question word goes at the end. That's how spoken French asks most questions.
Vous êtes d'où ? | Where are you from? | You are from where? | | N | Casual: "Tu viens d'où ?"
Je viens des États-Unis. | I'm from the United States. | I come from the United States. | | N | de + city, du + masculine country, de/d' + feminine country, des + plural. | Je viens + de / du / des + [lieu] | de Chicago · du Canada · d'Angleterre · de France
J'habite à… | I live in… | | | N | à + city, en + feminine country, au + masculine country. | J'habite + à / en / au + [lieu] | à Paris · en France · au Canada · aux États-Unis
Je suis américain(e). | I'm American. | | | N | No article before nationality or job: "Je suis médecin", not "un médecin".
Qu'est-ce que vous faites dans la vie ? | What do you do for a living? | What do you do in life? | | N | Casual: "Tu fais quoi dans la vie ?"
Je travaille dans… | I work in… | | | N | | Je travaille dans + [secteur] | l'informatique · la finance · la santé · l'enseignement
Je suis ici pour… | I'm here for… | | | N | | Je suis ici pour + [nom / infinitif] | le travail · les vacances · voir des amis
Je suis en vacances. | I'm on vacation. | I am in holidays. | | N
J'ai trente ans. | I'm thirty. | I have thirty years. | | N | Age uses avoir (to have), never être. "Je suis trente" means nothing.
Je suis marié(e). | I'm married. | | | N | Also: "Je suis célibataire" (single), "J'ai un copain / une copine" (I have a boyfriend / girlfriend).
Je te présente… | This is… / Let me introduce… | I present to you… | | N | Formal: "Je vous présente…" | Je te présente + [personne] | mon mari · ma collègue · mon ami Paul
On se tutoie ? | Shall we use "tu"? | We "tu" each other? | | N | Ask before switching from "vous" to "tu". Usually the older or more senior person offers.

@ 1 | noyau | Core frames: the engines of the language
Je voudrais… | I'd like… | I would want… | | N | THE polite request. Use it for anything you ask a stranger for. | Je voudrais + [nom / infinitif] | un café · réserver une table · essayer ça · un renseignement
J'aimerais… | I'd like to… / I'd love to… | I would love… | | N | Warmer than "je voudrais", often for wishes and plans. | J'aimerais + [infinitif] | visiter Lyon · te revoir · apprendre à cuisiner
Est-ce que je peux… ? | Can I…? | Is it that I can…? | | N | "Est-ce que" turns any sentence into a question. | Est-ce que je peux + [infinitif] ? | payer par carte · m'asseoir ici · essayer
Vous pouvez… ? | Could you…? | You can…? | | N | Asks someone to do something. More polite: "Pourriez-vous… ?" | Vous pouvez + [infinitif] ? | m'aider · prendre une photo · fermer la porte
Il y a… | There is… / There are… | It there has… | Y'a… | N | One of the most used phrases in French. As a question: "Il y a… ?" | Il y a + [nom] | un problème · du monde · un métro ici ?
Il n'y a pas de… | There's no… / There isn't any… | | Y'a pas de… | N | After a negative, un / une / des become "de". | Il n'y a pas de + [nom] | pain · réseau · place · problème
C'est… | It's… / That's… | | | N | Reacts to almost anything. | C'est + [adjectif] | bon · cher · parfait · trop loin · bizarre
Ce n'est pas… | It's not… / That's not… | | C'est pas… | N | Spoken French drops the "ne". | C'est pas + [adjectif] | cher · loin · grave · possible
J'ai besoin de… | I need… | I have need of… | | N | | J'ai besoin de + [nom / infinitif] | d'aide · dormir · d'un taxi · réfléchir
Je dois… | I have to… / I must… | | | N | | Je dois + [infinitif] | partir · travailler demain · y aller
Il faut… | You need to… / We have to… | It is necessary… | Faut… | N | Impersonal and used constantly. "Faut y aller !" = We've got to go! | Il faut + [infinitif] | réserver · y aller · attendre
Je vais… | I'm going to… | | | N | The near future: aller + infinitive. The easiest future tense, and the one people actually use. | Je vais + [infinitif] | manger · l'appeler · rentrer · réfléchir
Je viens de… | I just… / I've just… | I come from… | | N | The recent past: venir de + infinitive. | Je viens de + [infinitif] | arriver · finir · le voir
Je veux… | I want… | | | N | Direct. Fine with friends, can sound demanding with strangers. Use "je voudrais" there.
Je ne sais pas. | I don't know. | | Chais pas. | N | "Je sais pas" squashes to "chais pas". Savoir = know a fact or how to do something.
Je connais… | I know… (a person / place) | | | N | Connaître = be familiar with people, places, things. Savoir = know facts. | Je connais + [personne / lieu] | Paris · ton frère · un bon resto
J'aime bien… | I like… | I love well… | | N | Trap: with people, "je t'aime" = I love you, but "je t'aime bien" = I like you (as a friend). | J'aime bien + [nom / infinitif] | le jazz · cuisiner · ce quartier
Je n'aime pas… | I don't like… | | J'aime pas… | N | | J'aime pas + [nom / infinitif] | ça · attendre · le froid
Je préfère… | I prefer… | | | N | | Je préfère + [nom / infinitif] | le thé · rester ici · qu'on y aille demain
Ça me plaît. | I like it. | That pleases me. | | N | A very natural way to say you like something. Question: "Ça te plaît ?"
J'ai envie de… | I feel like… / I want to… | I have desire of… | | N | Very common, very natural. | J'ai envie de + [infinitif / nom] | dormir · sortir · une glace · voyager
Ça te dit de… ? | Do you feel like…? / How about…? | Does that say to you…? | | Fam | The casual invitation. | Ça te dit de + [infinitif] ? | aller au ciné · manger ensemble · venir
On peut… ? | Can we…? | Can one…? | | N | In speech "on" replaces "nous" for "we", and takes the "il" verb form. | On peut + [infinitif] ? | s'asseoir ici · commencer · payer
On y va ? | Shall we go? | One there goes? | | N | As a statement, "On y va !" = Let's go!
Je pense que… | I think that… | | | N | | Je pense que + [phrase] | c'est une bonne idée · il a raison · oui
Je crois que… | I believe… / I think… | | | N | A little less sure than "je pense".
Je n'ai jamais… | I've never… | | J'ai jamais… | N | | Je n'ai jamais + [participe passé] | été à Paris · mangé d'escargots · vu ça
Je n'ai plus… | I'm out of… / I don't have any more… | I have no longer… | J'ai plus… | N | ne… plus = no longer. | Je n'ai plus de + [nom] | batterie · temps · argent
Ça fait… que… | It's been… since… / I've been… for… | That makes… that… | | N | Uses the PRESENT tense: "Ça fait deux ans que j'habite ici" = I've lived here for two years. | Ça fait + [durée] + que… | trois mois · longtemps · une heure
Depuis combien de temps… ? | How long have you…? | Since how much time…? | | N | Also takes the present: "Depuis combien de temps tu habites ici ?"
